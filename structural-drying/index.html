Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

The structural drying technicians at Cleveland Restoration Squad are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. Using this knowledge, they calculate the specific equipment placement, temperature settings and airflow patterns needed to create the right evaporation conditions for each drying situation. Plaster, concrete, hardwood and drywall each dry at their own rate, so each needs its own approach to get down to an acceptable moisture level with no added damage.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

When to Call for Structural Drying in Cleveland

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try
  • Condensation forming on windows in the area that was wet
  • Moisture meter readings above normal in walls, floors or ceilings after water damage
  • No visible water left, yet walls or floors still feel cold and damp
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel
  • Gaps opening up in a hardwood floor, or planks with raised edges or centers
  • Earlier water damage that was never professionally dried and now shows deterioration

Why Professional Structural Drying Matters

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. The leading cause of secondary mold after a water event is setting out a few fans and trusting they will be enough. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

The equipment used for professional structural drying is fundamentally different from consumer products. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.

Maintenance Tips for Structural Drying

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Dehumidifiers lose efficiency when outside air gets in, so keep drying areas closed up
  • Do not place personal items on top of drying equipment or block the airflow from air movers
  • Each unit is positioned according to the moisture map, so ask before shifting anything
  • Deal with water intrusion right away, since an early start on drying gives a better result
  • Moisture can sit deep in a material whose surface feels dry, so rely on the meter readings
  • Daily monitoring only works if someone can let the technician into the property
