Few problems rattle a homeowner like finding the basement under water. Whether the cause is a failed sump pump, foundation seepage, a burst pipe, a water heater failure or heavy rain overwhelming your drainage, water in the basement needs immediate professional attention to prevent structural damage, mold growth and the loss of belongings. Cleveland Restoration Squad provides 24/7 emergency basement water removal throughout Cleveland, using powerful extraction equipment and advanced drying technology to get your basement dry and safe as quickly as possible.

Drying a basement is its own challenge: the space is below ground level, air barely circulates and dampness lingers. Between poured concrete underfoot, concrete block on every side and wet soil outside, water has nowhere to go and evaporation on its own is slow. To handle that, our crew uses equipment made for the job, including submersible pumps where the water is deep, truck-mounted units when there is a lot of it, and carefully placed dehumidifiers with air movers built for drying below ground.

Beyond removing the water, we address the root cause of your basement flooding to help keep it from happening again. Window wells, wall penetrations, exterior grading, foundation drains and sump equipment are all inspected so nothing that played a part gets missed. We then recommend long-term solutions such as a backup sump pump, an interior drainage system or waterproofing measures that protect your basement from future water intrusion.

Water extraction typically takes 4 to 8 hours. Complete structural drying of a basement usually takes 4 to 7 days, because concrete holds moisture and the space sits below grade. Readings are taken every day, and the equipment stays in place until the basement meets the dry standard.
Do not step into the water, above all when it has reached appliances or outlets. If the breaker panel can be reached on dry ground, switch off power to the basement. Carpet can often be saved if the water is clean (Category 1) and drying begins within 24 to 48 hours. Plan on new padding, though, since the old pad almost never survives. For health reasons, any carpet that sat in Category 2 or 3 water, including sewage, cannot be kept.

Why Hire a Professional for Basement Water Removal?

Removing water from a basement calls for know-how and equipment well past the consumer fans and pumps most homeowners own. Rental units are far slower than professional submersible pumps, and only commercial drying equipment sets the airflow, temperature and humidity required to dry a space below ground, where natural evaporation barely happens. Without proper professional drying, moisture left in concrete, wood framing and insulation leads to mold growth and deteriorating materials.

Basements also present their own safety hazards during a flood. Electrocution is possible whenever water reaches or covers outlets, wiring, the panel or plugged-in appliances. Natural gas lines, furnaces and water heaters can be compromised. Our technicians are trained to assess and mitigate these hazards before extraction begins, keeping your family and our crew safe throughout the restoration.

Maintenance Tips for Basement Water Removal

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Keep window wells clear of debris and consider adding window well covers
  • Clogged gutters dump water at the base of the house, so clear them out each season
  • Place leak sensors by the water heater and sump pit so you hear about water early
  • Install a battery backup sump pump to stay protected during power outages
  • Four times a year, check that the sump pump works by filling the pit with water
  • Extend each downspout so the water lands 6 feet or more from the foundation

Signs It Is Time for Basement Water Removal

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Basement flooring, carpet or tile that is discolored or breaking down
  • Standing water on your basement floor, whatever the source
  • Cracks in the floor, seams between wall and floor or gaps around pipes letting water in
  • Sump pump trouble: it never shuts off, never kicks on or sounds wrong
  • Leaks at the window frames below grade, or wells holding water outside them
  • Finished walls downstairs with bubbled paint or drywall that crumbles
  • Efflorescence, a chalky white residue, forming on masonry or concrete
